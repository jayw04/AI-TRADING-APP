## Ruling 1 — the frozen K5 quote-match rule reads **R2**

**Frozen §8 text:** `quote match = at-or-before, max age 5s`.

**The ambiguity.** Two readings were available, and they are not close:

| | Reading | Quantity bounded by 5s | Textual support |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R1** | snapshot's own freshness | `quote_age_s = cycle_ts − quote_ts` | Spec-pack §0.4 defines `quote_age_s` in exactly these terms; the quote record carries both fields |
| **R2** | gap back to the matched snapshot | `ref_ts − cycle_ts` | Spec-pack §2 honest-limit (a): the matched quote "can be up to the tolerance stale **relative to the fill**" — a sentence that only parses under R2 |

**RULING (owner, 2026-08-20): R2.** The 5-second tolerance bounds the interval from the reference
timestamp back to the matched snapshot's `cycle_ts`. A fill whose most recent at-or-before snapshot is
more than 5 s old has **no valid quote**, and per the frozen §8 population rule is excluded from **both**
the K5 numerator and the denominator.

**Pre-commitment discipline observed.** §8 states that *"no matching tolerance may be chosen after seeing
coverage."* Accordingly the developer session raised the ambiguity **without computing either coverage
figure**, and the ruling was made purely as a reading of the frozen text. Only after the ruling was any
matching arithmetic performed. This ordering is the substance of the rule, not a formality.

### 1.1 Measured consequence — the naive structural estimate was wrong by ~7×

The first-order expectation was that R2 makes K5 hopeless: snapshots are 60 s apart, so a fill at a
uniformly random instant matches only `5/60 ≈ 8.3%` of the time, far below K5's ≥90%.

**That reasoning is wrong, because the two grids are not independent.** The sampler's slot grid is anchored
at `HH:MM:00`, and the strategies' scheduled rebalances fire on the same minute boundaries. Measured over
the trailing 30 days (in-universe fills, `n = 117`):

| Reference timestamp | Within 5 s of a grid slot | vs. uniform (8.3%) |
|---|---|---|
| `created_at` (decision) | **66.7%** | 8.0× |
| `submitted_at` | **65.8%** | 7.9× |
| `filled_at` | **54.7%** | 6.6× |

Offset-into-minute histogram for `filled_at` (5 s buckets) is sharply front-loaded:
`{0–5: 64, 5–10: 17, 10–15: 4, 15–20: 4, 20–25: 4, 25–30: 3, 30–35: 5, 35–40: 4, 40–45: 1, 45–50: 5, 50–55: 4, 55–60: 2}`.

⚠ **These figures are a feasibility estimate and are INADMISSIBLE to K5.** They are drawn from the 30 days
*preceding* D0, i.e. outside the review window and outside the admissible corpus. They are recorded here to
show that the ruling's consequence was checked rather than assumed — not as evidence toward any criterion.

### 1.2 What actually binds K5 under R2 — and a vacuity the owner should see

Under R2 the binding constraint is **not** the ≥90% ratio. Because no-quote fills leave *both* numerator and
denominator, every fill that survives into the denominator has a valid quote by construction, so the ratio
approaches 100% and can realistically fail only if metric *computation* breaks on a fill that **has** a
valid quote.

The constraint that actually bites is **`N_min` = 50** on the surviving population. Rate estimate (again
inadmissible, pre-window): ~117 in-universe fills / 30 days ⇒ ~234 over the 60-day window ⇒ at a ~55%
match rate, **~128 matched fills**, comfortably above `N_min`.

⭐ **Therefore, under R2, K5 is likely to be EVALUABLE and to PASS — but that PASS is close to vacuous.**
It would attest that the pipeline computes metrics when it has data, not that SIP delivers execution-evidence
coverage. Note this vacuity comes from the **numerator-and-denominator exclusion rule, not from the tolerance
reading** — it is equally present under R1, where coverage would simply be ~100%.

⛔ This is recorded as an **observation, not a proposed change**. K5's definition, its threshold, its
population rule and `N_min` are frozen for the life of MDQ-001, and a criterion is not retuned because it
turns out to be easy to satisfy. Per §8.1 and the P-2 precedent, a finding that a K definition was poorly
chosen is **preserved and versioned prospectively for a future cycle**, never revised mid-window. It is
flagged here so that a K5 PASS is read at the verdict with its true evidential weight.

**Cause:** the 2026-08-19 22:03–22:06 EDT rebuild/redeploy to `9e5cf65` added a 1.43 GB image plus
**3.19 GB of reclaimable build cache** on a 30 GB volume. Free space on the morning of D0 had been 11.7 GB.

**Remediation (owner-approved in session):**

   `range639-20260818b`), all 5 containers up, capture root untouched. Build cache is regenerable and holds
   no evidence.
2. **Root EBS volume `vol-0457fe650ba7fd66a` expanded 30 → 60 GB** (gp3, `modify-volume` issued
   2026-08-20T13:31:17Z). `growpart` + `resize2fs` are deferred to **after the 16:45 ET freeze**, so the

⭐ **Standing lesson: after any redeploy, check free space before the next 09:25 ET sampler.** The deploy and

## Finding C — the holdout artifact's period field was never stamped (recorded, not silently accepted)

Discovered 2026-08-20 while implementing the DISC-MDQ-001 pre-read embargo (§4.13 PX-6).

`apps/backend/config/mdq_phase_a_holdout.json` still carries:

```json
"period_holdout_dates": "STAMPED_AT_FIRST_ADMISSIBLE_CAPTURE"
```

D0 occurred on **2026-08-19** and the concrete period holdout (**2026-10-06 … 2026-10-17 inclusive**) was
stamped in `MDQ-001_ProgramStart_Record_v0_2.md` and in the implementation plan — but **this
machine-readable artifact was never updated**. The period holdout is therefore not readable from the
artifact that exists precisely to make it machine-enforceable.

**Temporary behaviour, recorded here deliberately.** The embargo does not guess and does not tolerate an
incomplete artifact silently. It:

1. derives the period holdout from the **frozen rule** ("final 12 calendar days of the 60-day review
   window") applied to the governed window `[2026-08-19, 2026-10-18)`, yielding
   `[2026-10-06, 2026-10-18)` — which matches the pre-registered expectation exactly;
2. records the provenance string `derived_from_governed_review_window_artifact_unstamped` on every
   authorized scope, so no downstream artifact can claim artifact-backed enforcement it did not have;
3. **cross-checks and fails fatally** if a future artifact version states concrete dates that disagree
   with the rule — two governing sources disagreeing about what is quarantined is not a condition to
   paper over;
4. carries a **deliberately failing regression test** asserting the artifact is currently unstamped.

**Owner disposition (2026-08-20).** The fail-closed derived-date behaviour is the correct temporary
state and is recorded by this document. It is explicitly **not** a licence to tolerate both forms
indefinitely. When the artifact is properly stamped, that is a governance change, and **the deliberately
failing regression test is updated in the same act** — the test is the tripwire that forces the update to
be deliberate rather than incidental.

⛔ This is a document-state gap, not a control gap: the ten symbol holdouts and the derived period are
both enforced today, before any corpus file is opened.
